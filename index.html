Horae Diei

    About

    Romans split the daylight, from sunrise to sunset, into twelve equal horae, and the night into twelve more, grouped into four watches (vigiliae). So an hour stretched in summer and shrank in winter. By day this page draws a hemicyclium, the hollowed-stone sundial Vitruvius credits to Berosus, laid out for your latitude: the curves mark the winter solstice, equinox and summer solstice, and the dashed line is today's path of the shadow. By night, when a sundial is useless, it switches to the water clock of Ctesibius: water drips at a steady rate into a cylinder, and a float lifts a figure whose wand points to the hour.
